# Push

Phones not running Aspen are woken through their app's push relay or a UnifiedPush distributor, as `spec/push.md` describes; the relay, which alone holds a published app's Apple and Google credentials, is its own repository (`aspen-push-relay`). Every push is Web Push: a pointer (`app::push::Pointer`: `message`, `read`, or `deleted`, naming a channel and a message, and a `badge` count) encrypted to the phone (RFC 8291, `app::push::webpush`, the `aspen_webpush` crate, checked against RFC 8291's own example) and signed with the deployment's push key (RFC 8292), each audience's token reused until half its life is left and at most 1024 kept (`webpush::MAX_TOKENS`, letting go of those nearest expiry), since endpoints' origins are subscribers' to choose, a P-256 key in `push_key` made by the first server to start, like the federation key, and advertised as `push.applicationServerKey` in `GET /auth/methods`. `POST /users/@me/push-subscriptions` registers the calling sign-in's phone (`push_subscription`, one per refresh token, which cascades, so signing out stops it), keeping at most ten per account (`MAX_SUBSCRIPTIONS_PER_USER`): each registration, made one at a time per account under a lock on its `user` row, lets go of those of expired sign-ins and of the oldest beyond ten, so a message costs a bounded number of pushes per person however often they sign in; only phones of live sign-ins, of accounts neither deleted nor banned from the deployment, are woken (`phones_of`), so a sign-in revoked by a password change or a ban stops its phone too, and the badge counts tags only in channels the person may view; pushes go to HTTPS endpoints at public addresses only, through the same client as calls to other deployments, which resolves names to public addresses alone, while an endpoint that names an address rather than a host is checked itself (`reaches_inside`), refused when registered and its subscription dropped before a push, and only the first 4 KiB of a refusal is read, and a subscription whose endpoint answers `403`, `404`, or `410`, or made for a key since replaced, is dropped. What wakes whom is decided by one task per API server (`app::push::spawn_dispatcher`) reading the event stream through the durable JetStream consumer `aspen_push` they share, so each event is handled once, each DM copy deduplicated in Valkey; handling that fails, which it does only before it wakes anyone, lets go of its Valkey claim and is delivered again up to three times in all: a new message wakes the other people of its DM, or those it tags (by name, a role, or everyone) who may view its channel, never its author, anyone who blocked them here, anyone who muted the channel (a thread counting as its parent), or anyone active on Aspen at that moment; each is sent `message` with `Urgency: high`, up to `FAN_OUT` people prepared at once, their phones read in one query. The dispatcher never waits for a push service: once it has decided an event's pushes it queues them (`app::push::queue`) and acknowledges the event, and each push is a task of its own that waits for one of 256 slots of its endpoint's origin and then one of the server's 1024, and is given three seconds (`SEND_TIMEOUT`) to be answered, so a slow or unreachable push service delays only its own pushes. Up to 200,000 pushes wait on one server, queueing beyond that holding up the dispatcher until there is room, and up to 50,000 for one origin, beyond which that origin's pushes are dropped. A subscription whose push service has gone unanswered (no answer in time, or no connection) five times in a row is suspended, skipped until an hour has passed since the last, counted in Valkey (`push:failing:<subscription>`) so every server skips it, and any answer clears the count. `aspen_pushes_total` counts pushes by outcome and `aspen_pushes_waiting` those queued. Whom a message woke is remembered in Valkey for a week, so reading that channel elsewhere sends `read` and deleting the message `deleted`, both `Urgency: low`, which relays whose iOS app cannot hide a notification leave undelivered. Events are published before their transaction commits, so the dispatcher waits for the message, or the read position, to be there before it reads anything else, up to ten seconds (`COMMIT_WAIT`), looking again after 50 ms and then at doubling waits of up to a second; a message not there by then was rolled back, and one deleted meanwhile wakes no one. `scripts/dev_push.py` checks all of it against the development deployments, standing in for relay and phone.
