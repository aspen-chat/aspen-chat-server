# Blocking

A user may block anyone for themself alone (`app::block`, table `user_block`: the blocker and the blocked, both cascading). `PUT /users/@me/blocks/{user}` blocks (`201`, or `200` when they were blocked already), `DELETE` on the same path unblocks, and `GET /users/@me/blocks` lists them (at most 10,000 a person, `block::MAX_BLOCKS`, counted with the blocker's row held; one more is refused with a `validation` detail), most recent first, with `include=users`; each change is published to the blocker's own subject as the custom `userBlockChanged` event, and the blocked user is never told. Most of what a block does is the blocker's client's to do: it collapses the blocked user's messages into a row the blocker may open, and silences them and hides their screens in calls. The server does what the client cannot. No one-to-one DM is started or written in while a block stands either way: `channel_access` leaves both people only View channel there, so every write (messages, edits, reactions, threads, polls, votes, pins, calls) answers `403` `blocked`, which does not say who blocked whom, while the history stays readable and `POST /users/@me/dms` still returns a DM made before the block. No group DM is started with, or joined by, two people with a block between them, but one already holding both stays open to both. A blocked user's messages never make a channel unread for the blocker (`app::read_state`), and their reactions are left out of the blocker's summaries and reactor lists (`app::react`), so the blocker's counts may differ from everyone else's. Poll votes still count, since they decide the poll. The blocker's presence reads as `offline` to the blocked user everywhere it is given (`app::user_status::presence_visible`), and they are left out of every channel's count of who is online for the blocked user (`app::channel_presence`), so a block keeps them from watching when the blocker is about; the blocker still sees the blocked user's, which their client may hide.
