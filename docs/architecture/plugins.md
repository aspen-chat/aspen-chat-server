# Plugins

Plugins are WebAssembly components the operator installs (`app::plugin`, `api::plugin`,
`operator::plugins`), built against `spec/plugin.wit` and described by a manifest
(`app::plugin::manifest`, whose schema is `spec/plugin_manifest.schema.json`); `spec/plugins.md`
is the design and `docs/operators/plugins.md` the operator's guide. `plugins/word_filter`,
`plugins/forum`, `plugins/calendar`, and `plugins/blackjack` are example plugins, each built for
`wasm32-wasip2` in a workspace of its own: the word filter exercises intercepting, observing, and
most host calls, the forum a kind of channel and its view, the calendar timers, notices, cards,
and a capability URL, and blackjack a game whose table and players' chips are written by several
servers and its timer at once, through `storage-swap`.

**Installing.** Only the terminal installs (`app::plugin::install::install`): it checks the manifest (`Manifest::check`: a reversed-domain id, semver, interface version 1, every message key the manifest names present in its default language, settings fields well formed, permissions and what they need going together), compiles the component against the host's linker to prove it imports nothing the host lacks (`Plugins::check_component`), shows what it asks for, and writes the row of `plugin` (manifest, component, granted permissions, `mode`, `position`, settings), off. `dms` is granted only with `--grant-dms`. A manifest with a `principal` makes its account (a `user` row with `bot` set, no owner, and `plugin` naming it, which `app::bot::delete` refuses to delete) and publishes its commands as any bot's are. An upgrade replaces manifest and component, keeps mode, order, whether it is on, and the settings it still has, and asks again when it asks for more. `remove` turns it off, drops the component and its annotations (clients draw none for a plugin `GET /plugins` does not list, so these go without events), and takes its principal out of every community; `purge` deletes its storage, its communities' rows, and its settings, keeping the row (the record that it was installed) and its principal, which installing it again takes up. Settings, mode, order, and on/off are also the dashboard's under the deployment permission Manage plugins (`/admin/plugins`, `/admin/plugin-order`). Every change touches `updated_at` and is announced on the core NATS subject `aspen.plugins.changed` (`registry::announce`), on which every API server reloads (`Plugins::reload`), compiling a component again only when its SHA-256 changed.

**Running.** `app::plugin::registry::Plugins` holds the plugins that are on, compiled (`PluginPre`), in order, and caches each community's rows of `community_plugin` for at most a minute, forgotten at once when a community's use changes (the same subject, naming the community). `running_in_community` is every `everywhere` plugin and every `optIn` plugin the community turned on, each with the community's settings (defaults filled in); `running_in_dms` is those granted `dms`; a thread runs where its parent does. Each call first waits for a place among the calls running, one of its plugin's (`[plugins] concurrency_per_plugin`) and then one of every plugin's (`concurrency`; `Plugins::admit`), within its own deadline, and fails as any call does when none comes. A quarter of each set of places (at least one, where there are two or more) is kept for intercepting calls (`registry::Places`): a call that does not intercept also needs a place among the rest, so route requests and observers, which run for seconds, cannot take every place and make a message's intercepting calls fail, which a filter whose `failure` is `open` would let through. Each call is a fresh instance in a store of its own (`host::Instance`): memory capped at `[plugins] memory_mib` for all its memories together, and its instances, tables, and table elements bounded (`aspen_plugin_runtime::CallLimits`), and a deadline kept by the engine's epoch, which a thread of its own ticks every millisecond (`aspen_plugin_runtime::start_ticker`; the crate, `server/plugin_runtime`, also holds the bindings `spec/plugin.wit` generates and the engine); at each tick a call within its deadline yields to Tokio and one past it traps, and the whole call, host calls included, is cut off at the deadline (`host::within`). WASI is linked with nothing behind it (no preopens, sockets, environment, or arguments). Host calls live on `host::Call`, apart from the store's WASI state, since what they hold across an await must be shareable between threads.

**Intercepting.** `app::message::create_message` and `update_message` call `intercept::wanted` and, when any plugin running there intercepts the hook, check the author's permission to post first, and that every attachment is their own ready upload or, on an edit, already the message's (`check_posting`, `ensure_attachments_ready`), and then `intercept::decide`, before the saving transaction opens. An edit is decided whenever it changes the text or the attachments; one changing only the attachments shows the plugins the text as it stands, and a rewrite of it is saved as a change of text, its earlier rewriters still named. Each plugin sees the text the one before left; a rewrite needs `messages.rewrite`, may not exceed the longer of the text and 4000 characters, and may not tag anyone or anything the text did not (`tags_within`, by `mention::parse`); a refusal needs `messages.refuse` and becomes `pluginRefused` with the plugin's reason in the request's language. A call that fails counts as allowing or refusing (`pluginUnavailable`) as its manifest's `failure` says; a rewrite or refusal without its permission counts as a failure. A command is decided as the text it shows (`bot_command::invocation_text`, checked first by `bot_command::check`) with the files it takes, by `intercept::decide_unchanged`, which turns a rewrite into a refusal (`pluginWouldRewrite`), since its arguments are what its bot receives. `app::poll::create_poll` and `write_in` decide a poll (its question, then each answer on a line of its own) and a written-in answer the same way, under `message.create`, after the creator's or writer's permissions are checked. Reusing `message.create` keeps every installed plugin's filter covering polls without a new hook in the interface or the manifest. Warnings and the system account's notices are not intercepted, nor a principal's own messages by its own plugin. The plugins that rewrote a message are stored in `message.altered_by` and carried as `alteredBy`, an edit replacing them (its `Update` event carries the new set). Actions a plugin asks for while intercepting (`host::Deferred`) run once the hook has answered (`principal::run_deferred`).

**Observing.** Each plugin that observes anything has a durable JetStream consumer of its own (`observe::run`, named from its id), which every API server pulls from with explicit acknowledgement: an event its call fails on is delivered again a second later, three times at most, and only while the stream retains it (`MAX_EVENT_AGE`). A DM's events come once per recipient's subject; `first_copy` keeps, in Valkey, which subject's copy the plugin handles, so it is told once and a redelivery of that copy is still handled (events on a community's subjects come once, and need no such note). A message's `update` is told as `message-edited` when its text or its attachments changed. Message events are told with the message as saved (read after its transaction commits, retrying briefly), only where the plugin runs and with `messages.read`; `botCommandInvoked` reaches the plugin whose principal it names as `command-invoked`; a `communityPlugin` event of its own reaches it as `plugin-enabled` or `plugin-disabled`.

**What the host answers, and as whom.** `host::Call` checks the granted permission of every call. Reads are made as the caller of a route (`Phase::Route`) and otherwise as the plugin's principal, never as the plugin itself: `read-message` goes through `channel_access` for that reader, and is refused where the plugin does not run (`running_at`), as are `place-of` and `kind-of` (a channel's type by its wire name, `ChannelType`'s `Display`, and `plugin_type`), so a plugin answers only for channels of the kinds it serves; the example plugins each check the kind before reading or writing a channel's storage. Attachments are readable when they belong to what the call was shown, or, in a route, to a message the caller may read, and only up to `attachmentLimit`, which bounds what is read from storage: an object storage says is larger is refused before any of it is read, and one that runs past the limit is dropped as it is read (`MediaStore::copy_object_to`). `fetch` reaches only the manifest's hosts, over HTTPS, through a client that resolves public addresses only and follows no redirects, refusing a host that is itself an address inside a network (`outbound::names_inside_address`), which the client would connect to without resolving, never while intercepting, with bodies of at most 4 MiB. Storage (`storage`) is keyed by plugin, scope, and key in `plugin_storage`; `storage-swap` writes or deletes a value only while it holds what the caller expected (`storage::swap`), comparing and writing under the row's lock in one transaction, and a write that finds a key absent and loses the race to make it is tried again (or, for a swap expecting it absent, refused); a scope must be where the plugin runs, and in a route where the caller may look (a channel they may view, a community they belong to, themselves). `plugin.storage_bytes` counts keys and values against `storageQuota`, changed only when a value's length does. `forget` deletes a scope's values (a channel's with its threads', a community's with its channels') from `delete_channel`, `delete_community`, and `user::retire`. Counters (`counter-add`) are Valkey keys per plugin, window, and key, expiring with their window. Actions (`act`) run as the principal through `app::message`, `app::react`, `app::role::remove_member`, and `app::ban::ban_member`, inside `settle_after` as a request's would, so one cut off at the deadline part way is still settled as failed, and are refused while the principal is banned from the deployment.

**Annotations and events.** `messageAnnotation` records are published with `EventScope::Message` (the message's channel, and its `Aspen-Channel`) and sideloaded with `include=annotations` as `included.messageAnnotations`, read only for messages the read already allowed. `userAnnotation` records are published with `EventScope::UserEverywhere`, as a profile is, and read with `GET /users/{user}/annotations`. One of each kind per plugin and subject; setting one again is an `update`. `pluginEvent` carries the plugin's kind and JSON payload (16 KiB at most) to a channel's viewers, a community, or one user, and `expected_kind` routes it by which of `channel` and `community` it names. A `communityPlugin` event (a community turning a plugin on or off, or configuring it) carries `Aspen-Requires: managePlugins`, so only the community's managers receive its settings.

**Routes.** `/api/v1/plugins/{plugin}/routes/{*path}` (`api::plugin::route`) is outside the OpenAPI document and rate limited by `api::rate_limit::PLUGIN_ROUTE` (`plugin_routes` in `rate_limits.toml`, per user per plugin and per plugin). A body is at most 64 KiB and an answer 1 MiB. An answer is served on the API's origin, so a content type a browser would render as a document or run as script becomes `application/octet-stream`, and every answer carries `Content-Security-Policy: default-src 'none'; sandbox` and `X-Content-Type-Options: nosniff`.

**Channel types and views.** A plugin holding `channelTypes` declares kinds of channel (`channelTypes` in its manifest), each shown by a page among its `assets`, which installing reads from the manifest's directory (`asset::read_dir`: the kinds of file a page uses, 2 MiB each and 20 MiB together, and every kind's view among them) into `plugin_asset`, and every server holds in memory with the plugin. A channel of a kind has `ty` `plugin` and `plugin_type` naming it (`org.example.forums:board`); `POST /channels` makes one where a plugin that declares the kind runs (`channel_type::check`), with Manage channels as any channel, and posting a message to one is refused (`pluginChannelHasNoMessages`). Its contents are the plugin's storage scoped to the channel, so View channel, overrides, and its deletion govern them as they govern a channel's messages. The files are served anonymously at `/api/v1/plugins/{id}/assets/{*path}` (`api::plugin::asset`) with `asset::VIEW_POLICY`: a CSP sandbox without `allow-same-origin`, which gives a page an opaque origin however it is opened, scripts, styles, pictures, and fonts of its own or inline, and no connection anywhere. The app shows the page in a frame sandboxed the same way, and the bridge described in `spec/plugins.md` is its only way to the plugin, whose routes the app calls as the person. `GET /plugins` gives each kind's `pluginType`, name, glyph, and view.

**Timers.** `plugin_timer` holds each plugin's timers (at most `timer::MAX_TIMERS`), set and cancelled by key. Every server looks each second for due timers of plugins that are on, hold `timers`, and observe `timer.fire`, claiming up to twenty at a time for a minute (`FOR UPDATE SKIP LOCKED`), and hands each to `observe::deliver` as `timer-fired`; one handled is deleted unless the plugin set its key again meanwhile, one that failed is tried again once its claim runs out, and one tried three times is dropped.

**Notices.** `notify` (`notice::notify`) tells someone of something in a channel where the plugin runs only while `channel_access` admits them and their settings for the channel (a thread's parent's) would tell them of a message that tags them: not muted, level not `nothing`. A channel of a plugin's kind may be muted and given a level as a text channel may (`app::channel_mute`, `app::notification_setting`), which is how its notices are silenced. It keeps the notice in `plugin_notice` for a week, publishes `pluginNotice` to the person's subject, and wakes their phones with the push pointer `notice` unless they are using Aspen (`app::push::notice`); `GET /users/@me/plugin-notices/{notice}` gives a phone the plugin's name and the text in the reader's language, while they may still view the channel.

**Cards.** `send-card` posts a message of the plugin's principal with a card (`card::Card`, kept in `message.card` and carried as the message's `card`, naming its plugin), and `update-card` changes or removes the card of a message the principal posted, announced as the message's update. `POST /messages/{message}/card/buttons/{button}` (`card::press`) takes reading the message (`channel_access`), and the plugin's running where it is, and calls the plugin's route `aspen/cards/{message}/{button}` as the presser (`route::answer_host`), answering with the plugin's answer.

**Capability URLs.** `capability-path`, while answering a route, gives the caller their URL for a name of the plugin's, the same each time (`plugin_capability`, keyed by its secret, which is kept so the plugin can show it again). `GET /api/v1/plugins/{id}/capabilities/{secret}` (`capability::follow`), anonymous and limited per address and per secret, calls the plugin's route `aspen/capabilities/{name}` as the person, refused once they are banned or deleted; every URL of a person's ends (`capability::revoke_all`) when they change or reset their password or sign out everywhere else, since one copied by whoever took the account is a credential like a sign-in, and the plugin hands out a fresh one when next asked; reading as them, the plugin answers only what they may still see. Routes under `aspen/` (`route::HOST_PREFIX`) are the host's alone: a person's request to one is not found, as is one whose path has an empty, `.`, or `..` segment or begins with `aspen` in any case, as it stands, with backslashes read as slashes, or percent-decoded again (`route::person_may_ask`).

**Catalogue and federation.** `GET /plugins` lists the plugins that are on, each with its name, description, community settings fields, the permissions its principal asks for, whether it runs in DMs, and its messages in the reader's language (a pseudo-locale made from its default language), from which clients draw annotations, `alteredBy`, and settings forms. The deployment's document and `GET /auth/methods` name each plugin that is on as a capability (`Protocol::with_plugins`).

**When access is given or taken away.**

Phase 3 answers the same questions this way. A plugin's channel is a channel: who sees it, its events, and the storage scoped to it is decided by View channel, and losing it ends the plugin's routes' answers about it as for any channel's storage. A view's files are the same for everyone and hold nothing of anyone's. A notice is sent only to someone who may view its channel when it is sent, and read back only while they still may. A card is part of a message, seen by whoever reads the message, and pressing it takes reading it. A capability URL answers as its owner, so it answers nothing once they lose what it shows, are banned, or are deleted. `scripts/check_permissions.py` checks each with the calendar. A game's table is a plugin's channel too: blackjack shows its table to whoever may view the channel and seats only those who may send messages there, keeps each player's chips in their own scope (`wallet:{community}`), which deleting the account deletes, and answers a route about a table they can no longer view with nothing; `scripts/check_permissions.py` checks that, and that simultaneous bets keep a table and its players' chips straight.

1. *Who can observe it, and by which routes?* A message's annotations: whoever may read the message, through its channel's events and message reads' sideloads. A person's annotations: whoever shares a community with them, through their profile's events and `GET /users/{user}/annotations`. A community's use of a plugin: holders of Manage plugins, through `communityPlugin` events and `GET /communities/{community}/plugins`. A plugin's events: whoever its audience names, as any event of that scope. What a plugin keeps: through its routes, as the caller may look. The catalogue: every signed-in user.
2. *What decides it, and where is that checked?* `channel_access` in `host::Call::readable_message`, `scope`, `annotate_message`, and `read_attachment`, and for a route's caller in `publish` and `notify` (`caller_views`); `require_member` for community scopes and, in a route, a community's `publish`; a shared community, or being the person, in `annotation::of_user` for a person's annotations, which answers anyone else an empty list; the event feed's model for annotation, plugin, and `communityPlugin` events (by `Aspen-Channel` and `Aspen-Requires`); `Permissions::MANAGE_PLUGINS` in `app::plugin::community`; where the plugin runs in `Plugins::runs_at`.
3. *When the deciding permission is lost, what happens to what is already open?* Losing View channel (an override, a role, a move, a removal, a ban) stops the channel's annotation and plugin events reaching that stream from that point, as for messages; reads and routes check at each call. Losing Manage plugins stops `communityPlugin` events at once (the model's `Aspen-Requires`). Ceasing to share a community with someone (leaving, a removal, a ban) stops their annotations' events reaching that stream with the membership's end, and `GET /users/{user}/annotations` answers none once no community is shared. A community turning a plugin off, or the operator turning it off or removing it, stops its hooks on every server within moments (the cache is forgotten on the announcement), and its routes answer `notFound` once it is off; a community turning it off, or the operator removing it, also takes its principal out, whose role goes with it. A principal removed from a community, or stripped of its role's permissions, is refused its next action by the same checks a member's would be; one banned from the deployment is refused every action. Revoking `dms` (reinstalling without `--grant-dms`) stops its hooks in DMs. The client drops annotations of plugins `GET /plugins` no longer lists.
4. *When it is gained, how does a client already open find out?* A reader who gains View channel reads the channel's messages, and their annotations with them, as any channel gained. A member gaining Manage plugins reads `GET /communities/{community}/plugins` when they open the community's plugin settings. A plugin turned on appears in `GET /plugins`, which the client reads again when it meets a plugin it does not know.
5. *Does every path that changes it announce it?* Community changes publish `communityPlugin` events inside their transaction; the principal's joining and leaving publish its membership and role events; annotation changes publish their own events; the operator's and the dashboard's changes announce on `aspen.plugins.changed`. Removing a plugin deletes its annotations without events, and every client infers that from the catalogue.
6. *Is it published inside the transaction that makes the change?* Annotation, `communityPlugin`, membership, and role events are, so a rollback is answered by `communityResync` (and a membership's by `userResync` for its member too) as for any request.

`scripts/check_permissions.py` checks that a plugin's annotations and events stop reaching someone who loses View channel, that its route stops answering them for the channel's storage, that a community's plugin settings reach only holders of Manage plugins, and that turning the plugin off takes its principal out.
