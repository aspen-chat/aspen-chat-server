# Roles and permissions

Who may do what in a community is decided by roles (`app::permissions` resolves, `app::role` manages, `api::role` serves). A permission is one bit of `community_role.permissions`, and each has exactly one meaning; its wire name is the camelCase `Permission` enum, and a role's record lists names, never bits. Community permissions hold across the community: `manageCommunity` (rename, icon), `manageChannels` (create, edit, arrange, delete channels, and their overrides), `manageCategories` (the same for categories), `createInvites`, `manageInvites` (edit or revoke anyone's, and see them all; everyone may manage their own), `manageRoles`, `assignRoles`, `removeMembers`, `manageMessages` (delete anyone's, take down write-ins), `pinMessages`, `manageCalls` (server mute, remove from a call), `addBots` (add a bot through its link; see Bots), `manageCustomEmoji` (add, rename, and remove the community's emoji; see Custom emoji), `banMembers` (ban and unban; see below), `changeNickname` (choose one's own nickname; see below), `manageNicknames` (clear others' nicknames), and `managePlugins` (turn the deployment's plugins on and off in the community and configure them, and read their settings there; see Plugins). Channel permissions can also be allowed or denied per channel and per category: `viewChannel`, `sendMessages`, `attachFiles`, `addReactions`, `startThreads`, `sendInThreads`, `createPolls`, `joinVoice`, `speak`, `shareScreen`, `useCamera` (a camera in calls), `mentionMembers`, `mentionRoles`, `mentionEveryone` (see Tagging), and `transferFiles` (see File transfers). Editing a message is its author's alone. Community permissions take bits 0 to 31 and channel permissions bits 32 to 62 (`Permissions::COMMUNITY` and `Permissions::CHANNEL`), leaving each group room to grow without moving the other; the sign bit is never used. A new permission gets the next free bit of its group, a name, a localized `permission<Name>` string, and a place in the templates.

Every member holds the community's everyone role (`everyone`, position 0, which cannot be renamed, moved, or deleted) and whichever others they are given (`community_member_role`, listed as `roles` on their `userCommunity` record). Their community permissions are the union of their roles'. In a channel, its category's overrides apply and then the channel's own (`channel_override`, `category_override`), each layer applying the everyone role's override first and then the member's other roles' together, denials before allowances, so an allowance for any of their roles beats a denial for everyone. Overrides never touch community permissions and cannot name them. Managing a channel (renaming, moving, or deleting it, or setting or clearing its overrides) takes viewing it besides Manage channels (`app::channel::managed_channel`, through `channel_access`), so a channel hidden from someone is not found to them and they cannot undo what hides it, and filing a channel in a category, as it is made or moved, takes viewing that category (`app::channel::ensure_category_of`, through `in_category`), since the channel takes its overrides; managing a category (renaming, moving, or deleting it, or its overrides) takes viewing it besides Manage categories (`app::category::managed_category`), since changing those overrides or deleting the category would uncover its channels.

A member learns of a category only while its own overrides, applied to what they may do across the community (everyone's override, then their roles' together), leave them View channel there (`app::category::viewed_category` with `app::permissions::in_category` for one, `Visibility::can_view_category` and `CommunityModel::can_view_category` for lists and the event stream); the owner and deployment moderators learn of every one. What its channels' own overrides allow does not reveal it: a channel someone may view in a category they may not is listed with its `parentCategory`, a category they do not know, which the client files at the top level and counts as denying View channel. A category hidden this way is not found to them: `GET /categories/{category}` and its channels, its collapse (`PUT`/`DELETE /categories/{category}/collapses/@me`), and managing it all answer `404`, and community reads leave it out of `include=categories`, its overrides out of `categoryOverrides`, and it out of `collapses`. Its events (`category`, and its overrides' `categoryOverride`) carry the `Aspen-Category` header and reach those it lets view it (see Event routing); an override's change reaches those who could view it before or after, so someone losing it hears of the change and lets it go (the client's store drops it and its overrides once they no longer give View channel), and someone gaining it hears of a category they do not have and reads the community again. Its deletion reaches those who could view it before, and its overrides, cleared with it, are not announced one by one but inferred from the deletion (`ModelChange::CategoryDeleted`, and the client's store dropping a deleted category's overrides), since the last override's going would otherwise show the category to everyone. Every change that decides it is a role's permissions, a role given, taken, or deleted, the owner changed, a deployment role, or a category override, each already an event the client widens or narrows on as for channels; removal, a ban, leaving, and sign-outs end what the member reads as for channels. A category is made with no overrides, so it is shown to every member until its overrides are set. A deployment moderator views every channel, so this takes none of their powers. Deleting a role takes it from its holders and takes its overrides with it, by the foreign keys, and only the role's deletion is announced: the event feed and clients take it from holders and overrides themselves. Deleting a category moves its channels out of it, each move announced, and clears its overrides, in the same transaction, so nothing a deleted category decided outlives it (a deleted category's overrides apply to nothing either way). A channel may be created with its overrides (`overrides` on `POST /channels`, `app::role::check_initial_overrides`), each checked as setting it afterwards would be and written in the same transaction, and they are announced before the channel itself, so a channel made private is never open to more people than they allow and its creation reaches only those who may view it. A thread takes its parent channel's permissions, posting in one takes `sendInThreads`, and an echo also takes `sendMessages` in the parent. Every recipient of a DM holds every channel permission there and no community permission, except that any recipient may pin.

Both this resolver and the client's (`client/packages/protocol/src/permissions.ts`) run the cases in `spec/permission_vectors.json`; change the three together. The community's `owner` holds every permission and is above every role. Only the owner may delete the community or hand it on (`PUT /communities/{community}/owner`), and the owner cannot leave until they have. A deleted community keeps its rows but counts for nothing wherever memberships are read: `app::community::live` is the subquery those reads keep to, so its members find nothing of it in search (`Visibility` drops it), share nothing through it for DMs or presence, are not held to the membership cap by it, and its event streams no longer read it (`app::events::memberships`); its invites read as not found and join nobody, and `app::community::add_member` refuses every way in, holding the community's row until the membership commits so a deletion beside it waits. Roles are ranked by position; a member may manage, assign, or take away only roles below their highest, remove only members whose highest role is below it (never the owner), and likewise server-mute or remove from a call only such members, and give a role or override only permissions they hold; giving someone a role takes holding every permission it allows, as making it would, so Assign roles never hands on more than its holder has, while taking one away takes rank alone (`app::role::set_member_role`; the client offers to give only such roles). Handing on is judged by what the member is in the community alone: managing, giving, and taking roles, reordering them, and setting overrides rank by their highest role (`CommunityAccess::require_role_above`, `role_rank`) and give only what their roles allow (`require_holds` reads `member_permissions`), so a deployment moderator who also holds Manage or Assign roles there reaches no further with them than their roles do; Moderate any community ranks them above every role only for what it takes away. Each change locks the member's `community_user` row before it is made and announces their whole list as it then stands, so concurrent changes to one member's roles are announced in the order they commit, each with every earlier one in it, and the event feed's view of the roles they hold is the database's. A new community gets everyone's role with the member template (every channel permission but `mentionRoles` and `mentionEveryone`, `createInvites`, and `changeNickname`), Moderator (adding those two, `manageInvites`, `removeMembers`, `manageMessages`, `pinMessages`, `manageCalls`, `addBots`, `manageCustomEmoji`, `banMembers`, and `manageNicknames`), and Admin (every permission), its creator as owner, holding Admin too. `aspen-chat-server communities unowned|set-owner <community> <username>` names an owner from the terminal, for a community made before owners were recorded or left with none, announcing it as a handover is (`app::role::set_owner`). When an owner's account is deleted (`app::user::retire`), each community they own passes to the member whose highest role ranks highest, the earliest to join among equals (bots, the system account, and anyone deleted or banned from the deployment left out), who is told so by the system account; a community with nobody left is left without an owner.

A role may have a hue (`hue`, 0 to 359 around HSV's wheel) and may be shown apart (`hoist`), both set with Manage roles on roles below the caller like any other field (`POST /communities/{community}/roles`, `PATCH /roles/{role}`), and announced as the role's update; everyone's role has neither (`everyoneRolePlain`, and a check constraint). A member's name is drawn in the hue of the highest role they hold that has one, but only within that community: its channels, threads, calls, and member list. A deployment role's hue (see Administration) shows everywhere and is drawn over any community role's. The server stores and sends only the hue; clients choose how light and how strong to draw it, so that every hue reads on every ground (the reference client's `theme/nameColors.ts`), and each install may turn name colours off. A message read sideloads `include=memberships`, its authors' memberships of the communities the messages were posted in (`app::community::read_authors_memberships`), so a client can colour an author outside the member sample without reading them one by one; whoever may read a channel's messages may see their authors' roles, as `read_community_member` already allows. Online holders of a role shown apart are listed under it in the member list, each under their highest, and come first in the sample, above. Neither grants anything: who sees a community role's hue is who sees its roles, its members and deployment moderators, through the reads and events above, and a member who leaves or is removed takes the community, its roles with it, out of their store.

A ban (`app::ban`, `api::ban`, table `community_ban`) removes a member as removal does and refuses them every way back in (`app::community::add_member`, which every join, invite, and bot addition passes through) with `banned`, whose detail carries the reason they were given, until its `until` passes or it is lifted. `PUT /communities/{community}/bans/{user}` bans (`201`, or `200` replacing a ban that stood) with an optional `reason` (at most `REASON_MAX_CHARS`), `durationSeconds` (at least a minute; absent, until lifted), and `deleteMessagesSeconds` (3600 or 86400: the person's messages from the last hour or day go with the ban, in the community's channels the banner may view and their threads, as deleting them one by one would allow, which takes Manage messages besides), `DELETE` on the same path lifts, and `GET /communities/{community}/bans` lists the standing ones; all take Ban members, banning ranks as removal does (only someone below the banner's highest role, never the owner, and someone who has already left may be banned by their id), and a deployment moderator's ban, lift, and deletion are logged (`banMember`, `liftBan`, `deleteRecentMessages`). `communityBan` events are on the community's subject and reach holders of Ban members; a ban that stood is announced as its deletion and then the new one.

A community that gains as many members as the deployment setting `everyone_mention_limit` (200; see Administration) loses Mention everyone from its everyone role, since a tag of everyone would then reach more people than it likely means to, and its owner is told so by the system account and may give it back (`app::everyone_limit`). It happens once per community (`community.everyone_limited_at`, which the migration set for communities already that large): after a join commits, the server that made it locks the community's row, counts its members, and acts only if no one has, so concurrent joins that cross the limit act once and a join that finds it crossed acts even when the one that crossed it could not. Channel and category overrides are left as they are, as deliberate choices for their channels.

Every member reads the member sample (100 members, `MEMBERS_PER_COMMUNITY`), which is all a large community ever sends its members. It is chosen by priority (`app::community::read_community_members`): first those with a connection, online or away (`app::user_status::connected_members`), and among them holders of a role shown apart, by the rank of their highest such role, even when they fill the sample; then the rest of the connected; then everyone offline, whatever their roles; each tier by when they last came online (`user.last_seen_at`, written as the presence key is set afresh). The caller's own membership is always among it. Presence is not announced, so the sample is as of its reading; a client reads it again, at a moment spread over ten seconds, when it hears that a role came to be shown apart or not, that one shown apart moved or was deleted, or that one was given or taken (`AspenSync`). `GET /communities/{community}/members` with `filter[name]`, `offset`, or `limit` searches every member by name instead, a page of at most 50 at a time, with each one's roles in `included.userCommunities` (`app::community::search_community_members`), and `GET /communities/{community}/members/{user}` reads one member's roles, which any member may, since whoever reads someone's messages may see what roles they hold (`read_community_member`); in a community larger than the sample only its owner, deployment moderators, and holders of Assign roles, Remove members, Manage channels, or Manage categories may search, so no ordinary member can list a large community whole. A read of one channel's contents checks `app::permissions::channel_access`, whose `ChannelAccess` nothing else can make, and the readers that list what lies in several channels (channels, calls, read states, mutes, notification settings, channel overrides) take an `app::visibility::Visibility` and keep only what it lets the caller view. Refusals: someone who is not a member, or may not view a channel, is answered `404`, as if it did not exist; a member missing a permission is answered `403` `forbidden`, whose detail names the permission. Community reads sideload `include=roles` (`roles`, `channelOverrides`, `categoryOverrides`), a channel's overrides only for a channel the caller may view; role, override, and membership changes are events on the community's subject, a channel override's routed by its channel's View channel like the channel's own events, before and after the change (see Event routing). Someone who may not view a channel receives none of its events and finds it in no list (see Event routing), and a call's join token carries `speak`, `shareScreen`, `useCamera`, and `transferFiles`, which the voice server enforces and the API server keeps current: every change to who may do what in a community rechecks the calls it touches, removing whoever may no longer be there and sending everyone else what they may now do (`app::voice::recheck`; see Voice). The client follows the same changes (`AspenSync`): a channel it hears of but does not hold is looked up and its community read again, as is a community after a role the caller holds is deleted, after they come to moderate the deployment, or on `communityResync`; leaving, removal, or a ban takes the whole community out of the store.

A member may go by a nickname in a community (`community_user.nickname`, `nickname` on their `userCommunity` record), shown there in place of their display name: in its channels, threads, calls, member list, and tags. `PATCH /communities/{community}/members/@me` sets it (trimmed, one to `DISPLAY_NAME_MAX_CHARS` characters), which takes Change nickname, or clears it with `null`, which takes nothing (`app::community::update_membership`); `DELETE /communities/{community}/members/{user}/nickname` clears anyone's own, and someone else's with Manage nicknames, only for a member ranked below the caller and never the owner, as removal ranks (`app::role::clear_nickname`); a deployment moderator may too (Moderate any community includes Manage nicknames), logged as `clearNickname` when the community's own permissions would not have allowed it. Nobody sets another's nickname. Losing Change nickname keeps the nickname a member has; they may still clear it, and moderators may. A nickname is announced as the membership's update on the community's subject, which every member and deployment moderator receives, the list position (theirs alone) left out (`for_community`); reads carry it on every membership (the member sample, member search, which matches nicknames and orders by the name the community shows, one member's read, and message reads' `include=memberships`), so whoever may see someone's roles in a community sees their nickname there, and nobody else does. It goes with the membership: leaving, removal, or a ban takes it, and the client's store with the community. A nickname may be reported (see Reports), and a review may clear it.
