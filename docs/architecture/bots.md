# Bots

A bot is a user with `bot` set (`app::bot`, `api::bot`): it signs in only with its token, which the server keeps as a SHA-256 digest (`bot_token`), begins `aspenbot_`, and is sent as the bearer token of every request and as the `sessionToken` of the event stream's `identify`, so everything a person may do through the API a bot may do too, under the same permissions and limits. It has no password, second factors, or sessions: password sign-in refuses bots, and `two_factor::Caller::ensure_person` refuses every security change and reauthentication. Bots join communities by accepting invites like anyone, or through their link (below). The person who made a bot owns it (`user.bot_owner`), and may make at most the deployment setting `bots_max_per_user` of them (see Administration), and none while `bots_enabled` is off, which leaves bots already made working. A bot acts for its owner: while either is banned from the deployment its token resolves to no one (`app::user_ban::shut_out`), and banning the owner closes the bot's streams and takes it out of its calls (see Administration). `GET` and `POST /users/@me/bots` list and make the caller's bots, the answer to making one carrying its token, which is never shown again; `POST /bots/{bot}/token` issues a new one, ending the old, and takes a recently verified sign-in (`reauthenticationRequired`), since whoever holds a stolen session could otherwise take the bot's token; `PATCH /bots/{bot}` makes it public or private; `PUT /bots/{bot}/transfer` offers it to another person (see Handing a bot over); `DELETE /bots/{bot}` deletes it; and its profile is changed with `PATCH /users/{user}` by the bot or its owner. Deleting an account (`app::user::retire`) leaves its bots working and ownerless (`bot_owner` cleared, announced as their `user` update); a holder of the deployment permission Manage deployment settings may delete those, and no one may issue them tokens. A plugin's account, its principal, is a bot no one owns with `plugin` naming the plugin (records carry it as `plugin`); it has no token, acts only for its plugin, and goes only with it (`botIsPlugin`; see Plugins). Records carry `bot`, `botOwner`, and `botPublic`, and clients mark bots wherever they are named.

## Handing a bot over

A bot changes hands only with its recipient's consent (`app::bot::offer_transfer`, `accept_transfer`). Its owner offers it with `PUT /bots/{bot}/transfer` naming a person of this deployment under the cap, which takes a recently verified sign-in, as issuing a token does, since accepting gives the recipient the bot; offering it again replaces the offer (one row of `bot_transfer` per bot), and the recipient is told by a notice from the system account, sent in English after the offer is saved (a notice that fails is logged; the offer stands). The offer lasts seven days (`TRANSFER_OFFER_DAYS`). `GET /users/@me/bot-transfers` lists the standing offers made by and to the caller, each with the bot, the giver, and the recipient; `DELETE /bots/{bot}/transfer` withdraws one (its giver) or declines it (its recipient); and `POST /bots/{bot}/transfer/acceptance`, by the recipient, makes them the owner and gives the bot a new token, answered to them alone, so the old one, which the giver may still hold, stops working: in one transaction the owner changes (announced as the bot's `user` update), the token is replaced, and `signInsEnded` closes the bot's event streams and takes it out of its calls, as issuing a token does. Accepting needs no fresh verification; it takes nothing from the recipient. An offer stands only while it is unexpired and its giver still owns the bot: one whose giver deleted their account (leaving the bot ownerless) or deleted the bot reads as gone, and acceptance is refused while the bot is shut out of the deployment (`user_ban::shut_out`, which a ban of its giver does), as it is when the recipient owns as many bots as they may. Nothing about an offer is pushed to either side but the notice; the web client reads the offers when Settings' Developer section opens, which lists those made to the person, whether or not developer mode is on, and the Bots dialog shows each bot's own offer with a way to withdraw it.

Who can observe an offer: its giver and its recipient, through `GET /users/@me/bot-transfers` alone, and the recipient through the notice. What decides it: the row's `from_owner` and `to_user`, checked in the query, and at acceptance that `from_owner` is still the bot's owner. When the giver loses the bot (deleting it or their account) the offer stops standing, and a ban of the giver refuses acceptance while it lasts; a password reset by email does not touch bot tokens or offers, and neither does a password change (a bot's token is its own credential, which only its owner's fresh verification reissues). When the recipient gains the bot, their client learns of it from the acceptance's answer and the bot's `user` update; the giver's client sees `botOwner` change on that update and drops the bot from its list. `scripts/check_permissions.py` (`bot_transfers`) checks that issuing a token and offering a bot ask for a fresh verification, that an offer withdrawn cannot be accepted, and that acceptance ends the old token and its stream.

A bot's link is the client page `/bots/{bot}/add?permissions=…`, which suggests permissions by name. It calls `PUT /communities/{community}/members/{bot}` with the permissions chosen, which takes the community permission Add bots (in the Moderator and Admin templates) and a bot that is public or the caller's own. Permissions given go on a role made for the bot (`community_role.bot`, `app::role::insert_role`), which takes Manage roles and Assign roles, and only permissions the caller holds; the role is the bot's alone, cannot be given to anyone else or deleted, and goes when the bot leaves (`app::role::delete_bot_role`, from `end_membership`).

A bot advertises the commands it answers, which people invoke by typing `/name` (`app::bot_command`, `api::bot_command`). It publishes one list, `PUT /bots/{bot}/commands` (by the bot or its owner), the same everywhere; a bot of another deployment publishes its list on each deployment it uses. The list follows `spec/bot_commands.schema.json`, which a test holds to the Rust types (`ASPEN_WRITE_SPEC=1 cargo test` writes it afresh), and is checked whole before it is kept in `bot_command_list`, a refusal naming what is wrong where: at most `MAX_COMMANDS` (100) commands of at most `MAX_PARAMETERS` (25) parameters, names of 1 to 32 printable characters of any script but whitespace and emoji, unique within the bot ignoring case, descriptions of 1 to 100 characters with optional translations by language tag, optional parameters last, and a `pattern` on `regex` parameters alone, of the subset every client reads alike (`compile_pattern`: no group flags or named groups, `\A`, `\z`, or POSIX classes, and small once compiled), which a value must match whole. Parameters are typed `userId`, `channelId`, `messageId`, `communityId`, `roleId`, `attachmentId`, `deploymentHost`, `react`, `any`, or `regex`. `GET /bots/{bot}/commands` reads a list, and `GET /channels/{channel}/commands` what a channel offers: the lists of the bots that can view it, the community's in a community channel and the DM's in a DM, a thread counting as its parent. A new list is announced as `botCommandsChanged` wherever the bot's profile would be.

Someone invokes a command with `POST /channels/{channel}/commands` (`bot`, `name`, `arguments` in order, and `attachments`, the files its `attachmentId` arguments name, uploaded first), which takes what posting a message there takes. It goes through `app::message::create_message` with the invocation, which `app::bot_command::check` checks in the same transaction: the bot can see the channel and answers the command (names compared ignoring case), the arguments are as many as it takes, and each is what its parameter's type says as the caller may reach it (a person who exists, a channel or message the caller may read, a community they belong to, a role of this community, a file sent with it and uploaded, a deployment's domain, one emoji, which the bot receives fully qualified, text of at most `MAX_ARGUMENT_CHARS`, or a match for the pattern), a refusal naming the parameter and why. The command is then the caller's message of kind `command`, its content the command as sent (`invocation_text`: people and roles as the tags a message names them by, anything else quoted where it holds whitespace) and `commandBot` its bot, which tags no one, fetches no link preview, wakes no phone, and cannot be edited; the bot alone hears of it as `botCommandInvoked`, published to its user subject in the same transaction, with the message's id, the channel and its community, who invoked it, and the checked, typed arguments. The bot answers as it answers anything, with messages.
