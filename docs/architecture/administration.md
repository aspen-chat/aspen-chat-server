# Administration

The Administration Dashboard is the deployment's console. What anyone may do across the deployment comes from deployment roles (`app::deployment`, with the roles themselves in `app::deployment_role`, tables `deployment_role` and `user_deployment_role`), ranked by position like a community's and holding deployment permissions: `viewDashboard` (the totals, growth, fleet, directories, and moderation log), `manageRegistrationInvites`, `manageVoiceServers` (the `/voice-servers` registry), `manageDeploymentRoles`, `moderateCommunities`, `manageBots` (deleting bots whose owners are gone; see Bots), `manageFederation` (the directory of other deployments; see Federation), `reviewReports` and `manageReportCategories` (see Reports), `banUsers` (bans from the deployment, below), and `messageAnyUser` (a DM with anyone, past shared communities and blocks; see Threads and DMs). A holder of Manage deployment roles creates, edits, reorders, deletes, gives, and takes only roles below their own highest, and gives only permissions they hold, from the dashboard (`/admin/roles`, `/admin/role-order`, `/admin/users/{user}/roles/{role}`, `api::deployment`). Only the terminal reaches the top role: `aspen-chat-server admin grant <username>` gives it (making an Administrator role with every permission but moderation when there is none), `revoke` takes every role, `list` shows who holds what, and `admin allow|deny <permission>` changes what the top role allows, which is how moderation is first granted (each announces the change to those it touches as the dashboard does, `deploymentAccessChanged`, and rechecks their calls, so the terminal needs NATS as the servers do): `admin grant`'s Administrator role holds every permission but `DeploymentPermissions::MODERATION` (Moderate any community, Review reports, Ban users, and Message any user). Community roles never reach any of this, since a dashboard anyone could open would let anyone mint the invites an invite-only deployment is closed by. The API is `/admin/*` (`api::admin`, `app::admin`), every handler taking `AdminUser`, which refuses anyone without a deployment permission with `403` `adminRequired` and hands the handler what they may do, each requiring its own permission (`forbidden` without it); `GET /users/@me/admin` tells a client what the caller may do and which roles give it, and a change reaches them as the `deploymentAccessChanged` event.

A deployment role may have a hue (`hue`, 0 to 359 around HSV's wheel; `POST /admin/roles`, `PATCH /admin/roles/{role}`, where `null` takes it away), in which the names of its holders are drawn everywhere, over any community role's (see Roles and permissions). Each user's `nameHue`, on the user record everyone reads, is the hue of the highest deployment role they hold that has one (`user.name_hue`); every change that can move it, a hue edited, roles reordered or deleted, a role given or taken from the dashboard or the terminal, recomputes it for those it touches in the same transaction and announces each change as an update of the user to everyone who sees them (`app::deployment_role::refresh_name_hues`). Only the hue leaves the dashboard: which roles someone holds stays the dashboard's. A user of another deployment carries this deployment's hue here, never their home's.

Moderate any community is the deployment's power over what its users post (`app::permissions::MODERATION`): its holder reaches every community and DM without belonging, views every channel whatever the overrides, ranks above every role but the owner, and may delete messages, attachments, reactions, and poll write-ins, remove members (never the owner), rename and delete channels and communities, and list anyone's DMs (`GET /admin/users/{user}/dms`); nothing else, so a moderator posts nowhere they could not already. Each use that the community's own permissions would not have allowed, and every reading of a DM by someone not in it, is written to `moderation_log` (`app::moderation_log`) and the server's log, and the dashboard shows it (`GET /admin/moderation-log`) to those who may view it, each entry with what its ids name as the log is read (`details`, one query per kind of record for a page: the community, the channel or DM and its people, the message and its author, the person acted on, and the emoji, file name, write-in, or new name), so the dashboard names things rather than showing ids. A moderator's event stream follows the communities they belong to, where it shows every channel; elsewhere they read over REST, and the client applies their writes to its cache itself. It reads the record of files offered in calls (`/admin/file-transfers`; see File transfers), the deployment's totals (`/admin/overview`) and their growth (`/admin/growth?range=threeMonths|sixMonths|oneYear|fiveYears|allTime`: users and communities at each day up to half a year, week up to two years, and month beyond, each counted from creation to deletion, summed from one pass over both tables), its users and communities (searched by any part of a name through `filter[name]`, served by trigram indexes; ordered by `sort`, a field `-` prefixed for descending, newest first by default; paged by `offset` and `limit`, 15 by default, because a page must follow whatever column sorts it, which the keyset parameters the rest of the API uses cannot), registration invites, and the fleet. Powers too strong for even a privileged web API, such as suspending rate limits, stay terminal commands.

With `[registration] invite_required`, `POST /users` takes `inviteCode` and refuses without one (`registrationInviteRequired`) or with one that is not usable (`registrationInviteInvalid`); `GET /auth/methods` says which, as `registrationInviteRequired`. A registration invite (`registration_invite`, `app::registration_invite`) may be used `max_uses` times, until `expires_at` if set, and not once revoked; it is redeemed under a row lock in the transaction that makes the account, so concurrent registrations cannot overdraw it, and the account records it (`user.registered_with`). Where invites are optional, a code is still recorded when it works and ignored when it does not. Administrators make and revoke invites at `/admin/registration-invites`, which lists every usable invite and, for a week after it stops working (`app::invite::STALE_AFTER_DAYS`), one that no longer does: stopped is the first of being revoked, being used up (`used_up_at`, set when the last use is taken), and expiring; the terminal's `invites list` shows them all. A community's invite list (`GET /communities/{community}/invites`) follows the same week for invites that have expired; a revoked community invite is gone at once; an invite-only deployment's first account needs one from the terminal (`aspen-chat-server invites create [--uses N] [--expires 7d] [--note …]`), after which its owner grants themselves administration.

The deployment's profile is how it presents itself: a display name and an icon, which the sign-in screens welcome people with ("Welcome to {name}, an Aspen Chat instance.", or "Welcome to our Aspen Chat instance." without a name, under the icon, or Aspen's without one). It is the one row of `deployment_profile` (`app::deployment_profile`, `api::deployment_profile`), which the migration that makes the table inserts and whose key, `singleton`, can hold nothing but true; either field is `NULL` when unset, and the icon goes to `NULL` when its icon row is deleted. `GET /deployment` answers it to anyone, signed in or not, with the icon as its record (`downloadUrl` included, since a signed-out client cannot read `/icons/{icon}`); `PATCH /deployment` changes it with merge-patch semantics for holders of Manage federation, under which the deployment's identity among deployments already sits. A display name is trimmed and from 1 to 64 characters, in any script; an icon must be one whose upload is confirmed (`deploymentIconMissing` otherwise). It sends no event: only the signed-out screens and the dashboard show it, and each reads it when it opens.

Fleet health (`app::fleet`) comes from heartbeats: every `HEARTBEAT_INTERVAL` (ten seconds) each API server writes its uptime, open event streams, requests and server errors per minute, resident memory, and database connections to the NATS key-value bucket `aspen_fleet`, whose entries expire after thirty seconds, so a server that stops drops out. The figures are counted where the matching Prometheus metrics are recorded; the heartbeat is how the dashboard sees every server without reaching their loopback metrics endpoints. Voice servers' health is their registry rows: enabled, capacity, and whether their last report is within `offer_silence_seconds`.

A ban from the deployment (`app::user_ban`, `user.banned_at`, with `ban_reason` and `banned_until`) is how a deployment stops someone using it at all, its own users and visitors from elsewhere alike. `PUT /admin/users/{user}/ban` bans (`201`, or `200` replacing a ban that stood) with an optional `reason`, `durationSeconds`, and `deleteMessagesSeconds` (3600 or 86400: their messages anywhere on the deployment, DMs included, from the last hour or day go with the ban, which takes Moderate any community besides), and for a bot `withOwner` to ban its owner too; `DELETE` lifts. Both take Ban users and rank by deployment roles: only someone whose highest role is below the banner's, never the banner or the system account. A ban revokes every sign-in they hold, publishes `accountBanned` (its reason and end) to their own subject, after which the event feed closes each of their streams (`FeedEvent::ends`, close code 4410), and takes them out of every call (`app::voice::kick_everywhere`; a join not yet reported is removed as its report is applied). While it stands (`user_ban::BANNED_SQL`: made, and not yet ended), every sign-in is refused at `app::login::issue_session` with `deploymentBanned` and the reason and end in its detail (a password sign-in learns so before any second factor is asked for), any token still held resolves to no one (a bot's included, which keeps its tokens for when the ban is lifted), and other deployments asking after them are told `refused` (see Federation). They stay in their communities, so their name and what they posted still show. Each ban and lift is in the moderation log (`banUser`, `liftUserBan`). The user directory takes `filter[banned]=true` and gives each standing ban as `ban`; holders of Ban users or Review reports may browse it as moderators may.
