# Activity feed

The activity feed is every message that tells a user of itself, from everywhere they read on a deployment, newest first (`app::activity`, `GET /users/@me/activity`); apps signed in to several deployments merge each one's feed. A message tells its reader by the same rule as their notifications (see Notifications and Push): in a DM, a community, or a channel, every message where their level is `all` (a DM's by default), only those that tag them (by name, a role they hold, or everyone) where it is `tags` (a community's by default), and nothing where it is `nothing`, a thread counting as its parent; and every reply in a thread they follow (see Threads and DMs), whatever their level. Never in a channel they muted (a thread's parent included), never by them or anyone they blocked, never from before they joined (the community, or the DM a thread's parent is), and never a message that only records something (an echo, a poll's result, a call, a command). The places are worked out in Rust from the user's memberships, `Visibility`, settings, mutes, and follows, and one query reads the page by message id, as search does. `filter[community]` (comma separated) keeps only those communities, `filter[dms]=false` leaves DMs out, and `filter[unread]=true` keeps only what is after the reader's position where it was said (a thread's own, see Read positions). Pages are `limit` (25, at most 50) and `before`, and take the same `include` names as any message read, `readStates` among them, which brings the read positions of the channels and threads the messages were said in. The feed reads what the reader may read now, so it needs no table of its own, and it has its own rate limit.

## When access is given or taken away

1. **Who can observe it, and by which routes?** Only the reader, through the one read; new messages reach an open feed through the event stream as any message does, the client deciding with `RecordStore.notifies`, the same rule.
2. **What decides it, and where is that checked?** `app::activity::read_activity` builds its places from `Visibility::visible_channels` and the DMs the user is in, so a channel they may not view is never searched; threads are read through their parents.
3. **When the deciding permission is lost?** Every way of losing a channel takes its messages out of the next read; an open feed stops hearing of the channel as the event stream stops carrying it, and its rows already shown are read on demand when the store drops them, and leave when refused. A sign-out, password change, or ban ends the session.
4. **When it is gained?** The next read holds it, and events of the channel reach the open feed from then on.
5. **Does every path that changes it announce it?** It is derived from what is announced elsewhere (messages, settings, mutes, follows, memberships), with nothing of its own to announce.
6. **Is it published inside the transaction that makes the change?** It publishes nothing.
