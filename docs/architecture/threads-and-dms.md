# Threads and DMs

A thread is a channel of type `thread` (`app::thread`): replies to one message of a text channel, DM, or group DM, made when it is first opened (`PUT /messages/{message}/thread`, `201` the first time and `200` after) and never before, so no thread is empty. Its record names its `parentChannel` and `starterMessage`, and the starter names it in `Message.thread`; both ends are written in the transaction that makes it, under a lock on the starter so two first openings make one thread. A thread in a community records the community too, so routing and history reads treat it like any channel, and every listing of a community's channels leaves threads out (`parent_channel IS NULL`). A message in a thread cannot start one, and neither can an echo. `replyCount` and `lastReplyAt` summarise the thread for its starter: every message posted to the thread, polls and poll announcements included, counts, kept exact under the thread row's lock and announced as the thread's `update` event. Posting to a thread with `echoToParent` also creates a `thread_echo` message in the parent channel: no content of its own, `echoOf` naming the reply, the reply's author as its author. It is a reference, not a copy: the client shows the reply's current content, deleting the reply deletes its echo in the same transaction, announcing the echo's deletion first so no client ever holds an echo whose reply is gone, and an echo's content cannot be edited. Message reads sideload `threads` (the starters' threads, as `included.channels`) and `echoes` (the replies echoes name, as `included.messages`).

DMs and group DMs are channels of type `dm` and `groupDm` that belong to no community (`app::dm`). Their people are rows of `dm_recipient` and ride on the channel record as `recipients`, empty for every other channel. `POST /users/@me/dms` with one other person returns their one-to-one DM, made on first use (`201`, else `200`); `channel.dm_key` holds the pair's sorted ids under a unique index, so concurrent first messages make one DM. With more people it always makes a new group DM, at most `app::dm::MAX_RECIPIENTS` (ten) including the caller. Group DMs gain people with `PUT /channels/{channel}/recipients/{user}` and lose the caller with `DELETE /channels/{channel}/recipients/@me`; a one-to-one DM does neither. Everyone in a DM must share a community with whoever started it or added them. A block between the two people of a one-to-one DM leaves it readable and nothing more, and no group DM may bring two such people together (see Blocking). Only recipients may read or write a DM or anything in it, its threads included: everyone else is answered `404` by every channel, message, reaction, poll, and thread endpoint (`app::permissions::channel_access`); no role or deployment power reaches into a DM. `GET /users/@me/dms` lists the caller's DMs, the most recently active first, and sideloads their people with `include=users` and their calls with `include=voice`. A DM or group DM holds a call as a voice channel does, through the same join, voice servers, and events (published, as everything in a DM is, to its recipients' user subjects); everyone in it may join, speak, share a screen, and send files, and no one moderates it, since moderating a call takes Manage calls, a community permission no one holds in a DM. A block between the two people of a one-to-one DM leaves neither able to join its call. A DM's call rings: when its first person joins (who becomes `voice_session.started_by`), everyone else in the DM is rung for `app::voice::ring::RING_SECONDS` (fifteen), a row of `voice_ring` announced as a `voiceRing` create; joining the call or declining it (`DELETE /channels/{channel}/voice/rings/@me`) ends their ring with its `delete`, and the call's end ends every ring of it. A ring that runs out ends at its `until` by every client's clock, with no event; the reaper only clears the rows, and reads never return a spent one. The DM list's `include=voice` brings the rings in force too, as `included.voiceRings`. A session that replaces one lost with its voice server keeps its start and its starter, so its people rejoining rings no one again. When a DM's call ends, other than by its server being lost, it leaves a message in the DM by whoever started it: of kind `call`, whose `callSeconds` says how long it lasted, or, if it never held two people at once (`voice_session.had_company`, carried over like the start and the starter), of kind `missed_call`, with no length; like echoes and poll results, it wakes no phone, notifies no one, and is left out of search, since the ring already told everyone of the call.

The deployment speaks to people through its own account (`app::system_account`): one `user` row with `system` set, made the first time it is needed and called by `[system_account] display_name`. It has no password or token, and its username (`system`) is outside `user_name_key` and every lookup by name, so it takes no one's name and nothing signs in as it. `system_account::notify` sends a notice as an ordinary message of its one-to-one DM with the person, which it alone may start and which needs no shared community, so push, unread counts, and search treat notices like any DM. The person reads them and nothing more (`ChannelAccess::from_system`, `403` with a detail saying they cannot be answered), and cannot DM, block (`systemAccountNoBlock`; muting quiets it), or add the account to a group DM. Records carry `system`, and clients mark the account as the system wherever it is named and offer no way to message, call, or block it. A notice names `@everyone` only as code, since the account holds every permission in its DM and a tag there would count.
