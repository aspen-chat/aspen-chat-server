# Threads and DMs

A thread is a channel of type `thread` (`app::thread`): replies to one message of a text channel, DM, or group DM, made by its first reply (`POST /messages/{message}/thread/messages`, `app::message::To::ThreadOf`) in the transaction that posts it, so replying is what starts a thread and a thread starts with its reply. That endpoint posts as `POST /channels/{channel}/messages` posts to the thread, with the same checks, plugins, echo, and holding; the reply's `channelId` names the thread, and once it is made posting there is the same, so clients post later replies to the thread itself. Making it takes Start threads, besides Send in threads for the reply. Its id is chosen before the plugins decide the reply, which they are told is in a thread of the parent (`intercept::Draft::unmade_thread_of`), and everything is checked before the transaction opens (`check_first_reply`), so a refused reply announces no thread; the transaction checks it all again. A reply held for its previews (see Attachment previews) makes the thread as it is held, and waits in it. When it is dropped and nothing was ever posted in the thread (no message, deleted ones included) and nothing else waits there, the thread goes with it, in the transaction that drops it (`thread::remove_if_unreplied`): its followers are told they follow it no longer (`threadFollowChanged`), the starter's `thread` is cleared and announced, the thread's `delete` is announced, and its row is deleted, taking its read positions, mutes, and notification settings with it, so the next first reply makes a new one. That transaction locks the starter, as making a thread does, and then the thread's row, which every posting in a channel holds from its check until it commits (`message::hold_channel`, `FOR KEY SHARE`, as the message's own reference would from its insert on; poll creation holds it too): a reply racing the removal is either seen by it, keeping the thread, or finds the thread gone and is answered `404`, and a first reply racing it makes a new thread. Waiting messages are found by `held_message_by_channel`. `PUT /messages/{message}/thread` (`open_thread`, `201` when it makes it and `200` after) also makes one with no reply, for clients that open a thread before replying. Its record names its `parentChannel` and `starterMessage`, and the starter names it in `Message.thread`; both ends are written in the transaction that makes it, under a lock on the starter so two first replies or openings make one thread, the later posting to the thread the earlier made. A thread in a community records the community too, so routing and history reads treat it like any channel, and every listing of a community's channels leaves threads out (`parent_channel IS NULL`). A message in a thread cannot start one, and neither can an echo. A thread goes with its parent: once the parent channel is deleted, `channel_access` finds neither, so the thread, its replies, and posting in it are all answered `404`, and search, which reaches threads only through their live parents, finds none of them. `replyCount` and `lastReplyAt` summarise the thread for its starter: every message posted to the thread, polls and poll announcements included, counts, kept exact under the thread row's lock and announced as the thread's `update` event. Posting to a thread with `echoToParent` also creates a `thread_echo` message in the parent channel: no content of its own, `echoOf` naming the reply, the reply's author as its author. It is a reference, not a copy: the client shows the reply's current content, deleting the reply deletes its echo in the same transaction, announcing the echo's deletion first so no client ever holds an echo whose reply is gone, and an echo's content cannot be edited. A reply's author may also echo it later (`PUT /messages/{message}/echo`, `201` when it makes the echo, `200` with the echo the reply already has), which takes Send messages in the parent channel as echoing on posting does; the echo is new there, timed when it was made, and only text a person wrote is echoed (`standard` and `command` replies). A reply names its live echo in `echo`, written in the transaction that makes the echo (the column's reference is checked at commit, since the echo is inserted after the reply that names it) and announced by the reply's `update` whenever it changes later. Deleting an echo alone clears its reply's `echo`, after which the reply may be echoed again; at most one live echo names a reply (`message_echo_of`, a unique index over undeleted rows). Echoing shows no one anything new: the echo is a message of the parent channel, read, routed (its events in the parent's scope, the reply's `update` in the thread's), and searched past as any echo, and it shows a reply whose thread its readers already read. Losing Send messages in the parent stops further echoes and leaves those made, as it leaves messages sent. Message reads sideload `threads` (the starters' threads, as `included.channels`) and `echoes` (the replies echoes name, as `included.messages`).

A user may follow a thread (`app::thread_follow`, table `thread_follow`: the user, the thread, each cascading, and when they last followed it), and every reply in a followed thread tells them, by push, by the apps' notifications, and in the activity feed (see Activity feed), whatever their notification level for the parent; a mute of the parent still silences it. They follow it by hand (`PUT /channels/{channel}/follows/@me`, `201` or `200`; `DELETE` to stop, which lasts until they follow again or take part again), or by taking part: the author of the message a thread starts from follows it as it is made, and posting in it, a poll included, or being tagged by name in a reply while able to read it follows it again and marks it followed now; bots, the system account, and deleted accounts follow only by hand, if at all. A follow of a DM's thread tells only the DM's people, whoever followed it before leaving. A user follows at most `MAX_THREAD_FOLLOWS` (1000) threads on a deployment, and past that those followed least recently are let go, each announced. `GET /users/@me/thread-follows` lists the threads they follow that they may read now; each change is published to their own subject as `threadFollowChanged`. A follow of a thread whose parent the user loses stays, and tells them nothing while they may not read it: push asks who may view the parent, the feed reads only what they may read, and the event stream carries none of it; the list leaves it out until they may read it again. Following and unfollowing are published inside their transactions, a rollback answered by `userResync`.

DMs and group DMs are channels of type `dm` and `groupDm` that belong to no community (`app::dm`). Their people are rows of `dm_recipient` and ride on the channel record as `recipients`, empty for every other channel. `POST /users/@me/dms` with one other person returns their one-to-one DM, made on first use (`201`, else `200`); `channel.dm_key` holds the pair's sorted ids under a unique index, so concurrent first messages make one DM. With more people it always makes a new group DM, at most `app::dm::MAX_RECIPIENTS` (ten) including the caller. Group DMs gain people with `PUT /channels/{channel}/recipients/{user}` and lose the caller with `DELETE /channels/{channel}/recipients/@me`, both only from someone in the group (a deployment moderator who reads it is answered `404`, as by `channel_access`); a one-to-one DM does neither. Everyone in a DM must share a community with whoever started it or added them, except that a holder of the deployment permission Message any user may start a DM with anyone, add anyone, and write in a one-to-one DM past a block either way, though no group DM brings two others with a block between them together. A block between the two people of a one-to-one DM leaves it readable and nothing more, and no group DM may bring two such people together (see Blocking). Only recipients may read or write a DM or anything in it, its threads included: everyone else is answered `404` by every channel, message, reaction, poll, and thread endpoint (`app::permissions::channel_access`); no role reaches into a DM. The one deployment power that does, Moderate any community (see Administration), reaches it only where a path asks for it by name: `channel_access_reading` for reading what is in it (its messages, one message, its pins, a poll, who reacted, an attachment, and messages sideloaded by `include=echoes` or `include=linked`), which writes the reading to the moderation log before anything is read, once per DM however many of its messages a read returns, and `channel_access_moderating` for taking something out of it or reading its record, each of which logs its own action. Every other path, search included, finds no DM a moderator is not in, so a new one cannot read a DM unlogged by forgetting to log. `GET /users/@me/dms` lists the caller's DMs, the most recently active first, a page at a time (`before`, the last DM of the page before, and `limit`, 50 by default and at most `MAX_DM_PAGE`, 100), and sideloads their people with `include=users` and their calls with `include=voice`, and, with `include=mutes` and `include=notifications`, the caller's mutes and settings of every DM they are in, listed or not, since those are their own few rows and a DM not listed yet must be known muted when it is heard from. How recently a DM was active is `dm_recipient.active_at` for each of its people: when they joined it, moved on by every message posted in it (the trigger `message_inserted`, which every insert of a message runs, also fills `message.home_channel`), so the list is read through `dm_recipient_by_activity` however many DMs someone has; a DM that became active since an earlier page moves up and is listed again rather than missed. `GET /admin/users/{user}/dms` pages the same way. A DM or group DM holds a call as a voice channel does, through the same join, voice servers, and events (published, as everything in a DM is, to its recipients' user subjects); everyone in it may join, speak, share a screen, and send files, and no one moderates it, since moderating a call takes Manage calls, a community permission no one holds in a DM. A block between the two people of a one-to-one DM leaves neither able to join its call. A DM's call rings: when its first person joins (who becomes `voice_session.started_by`), everyone else in the DM is rung for `app::voice::ring::RING_SECONDS` (fifteen), a row of `voice_ring` announced as a `voiceRing` create; joining the call or declining it (`DELETE /channels/{channel}/voice/rings/@me`) ends their ring with its `delete`, and the call's end ends every ring of it. A ring that runs out ends at its `until` by every client's clock, with no event; the reaper only clears the rows, and reads never return a spent one. The DM list's `include=voice` brings the rings in force too, as `included.voiceRings`. A session that replaces one lost with its voice server keeps its start and its starter, so its people rejoining rings no one again. When a DM's call ends, other than by its server being lost, it leaves a message in the DM by whoever started it: of kind `call`, whose `callSeconds` says how long it lasted, or, if it never held two people at once (`voice_session.had_company`, carried over like the start and the starter), of kind `missed_call`, with no length; like echoes and poll results, it wakes no phone, notifies no one, and is left out of search, since the ring already told everyone of the call.

The deployment speaks to people through its own account (`app::system_account`): one `user` row with `system` set, made the first time it is needed and called what the deployment is called (its display name, or "Aspen"; see Administration), which renaming the deployment renames; it belongs to no community, so no event of that reaches anyone, and those holding its record see the new name when they next read it. It has no password or token, and its username (`system`) is outside `user_name_key` and every lookup by name, so it takes no one's name and nothing signs in as it; and no one may take its name, or one that looks like it, for an account of their own, at registration, a rename, or making a bot (`app::user::validate_new_username`, which compares UTS #39 confusable skeletons of the names in compatibility form (NFKC) and in two foldings of case, through `unicode-security`: `SYSTEM`, `ＳＹＳＴＥＭ`, `ѕуѕtеm` with Cyrillic letters, and `systern` are all refused), so nobody passes for it. `system_account::notify` sends a notice as an ordinary message of its one-to-one DM with the person, which it alone may start and which needs no shared community, so push, unread counts, and search treat notices like any DM. The person reads them and nothing more (`ChannelAccess::from_system`, `403` with a detail saying they cannot be answered), and cannot DM, block (`systemAccountNoBlock`; muting quiets it), or add the account to a group DM. Records carry `system`, and clients mark the account as the system wherever it is named and offer no way to message, call, or block it. A notice names `@everyone` only as code, since the account holds every permission in its DM and a tag there would count. Notices quote names others chose (a community's, the person whose account was deleted), so nothing in a message of the system account's is fetched for a link preview (`app::message::create_message`) or shown as a link by the client, and a name written as a link or an address reads as the text it is.
