# Attachment previews

A picture or video uploaded to a message is shown inline at no more than 320 CSS pixels tall, while a phone's camera writes several megabytes of pixels it will never be shown at. So the server makes a preview of each (`app::attachment::preview`): a smaller copy of a picture, encoded for the web, and a poster for a video, one frame of it made the same way. The work done on the bytes, decoding, resizing, encoding, and running `ffmpeg`, is the `aspen_previews` crate's (`server/previews`); reading originals from storage, the queue, and keeping what is made are the server's. Readers' apps show the preview inline and the original everywhere else: the gallery, a video's player, and downloads.

An original is uploaded as `app::attachment` and `app::media_store` describe: the client declares the file's type and size (`byteSize`, required, at most `[media] max_attachment_bytes`), and the upload URL is signed for exactly that size and for one `Content-Type` (`contentType` on the handle). That is the declared type's essence, its parameters dropped but for a plain text's `charset`, when it is among `app::attachment::INLINE_TYPES` (pictures, video, and sound browsers play, plain text, PDF), and otherwise `application/octet-stream`, since the anonymous-read path serves objects with the type they were stored with and a browser opening an HTML page, an SVG, or XML from it would run what it holds. Confirming moves the upload into place with that type and a `Content-Disposition` (`inline` or `attachment`, with the file's name; SeaweedFS's read path ignores it and gives its own) and refuses, deleting it, an upload over the limit. It copies only the object it weighed, by its `ETag` (`x-amz-copy-source-if-match`), so a second upload to the same URL between the weighing and the copy is weighed afresh rather than copied unweighed. The record keeps the declared type, which apps show and which decides whether a preview is made. A message carries at most 50 attachments (`app::message::MAX_ATTACHMENTS`, checked with the rest in `ensure_attachments_ready` as it is sent, held, or edited; `messageTooManyAttachments`), and the message box uploads no more than that.

Each person may upload at most the deployment setting `upload_quota_gib` (25 GiB; 0 sets no limit) in any 24 hours, attachments and icons together (`app::upload_quota`). Starting an upload records the bytes its URL is signed for in `upload_usage`, under a lock on the uploader's row, whatever then becomes of it (confirmed, sent, deleted, or swept), so deleting files does not make room; an upload that would pass the quota is refused with `429` `uploadQuotaExceeded`, whose `detail` names the quota and how long until there is room for the file and whose `Retry-After` says the same in seconds (none when the file alone is larger than the quota). Records older than a day are pruned with the upload sweep. A file's name is at most 255 characters (`app::attachment::MAX_FILE_NAME_CHARS`) and its declared type at most 255 bytes (`MAX_MIME_TYPE_BYTES`) of printable ASCII with no commas or quotes (`declarable`), so nothing the uploader writes after the essence, such as `text/plain;x=,text/html`, can reach the header a browser reads as several types.

## What a preview is

A preview is fitted within `aspen_previews::BOX`, 1920 × 960 pixels, never enlarged, its aspect ratio kept (`aspen_previews::fit`): 960 is the 320 CSS pixels a picture may stand inline on a screen of three device pixels to the CSS pixel, and 1920 the widest message column at two. It is lossy WebP at quality 85 (`aspen_previews::picture::QUALITY`), with libwebp's sharp YUV conversion so that fine detail in saturated colour (red text on white) is not smeared; at that density it shows no loss. One with transparency keeps it; one whose alpha channel covers all of itself, as many screenshots' does, drops it.

A preview is kept only when it is at least `MIN_SAVING_PERCENT`, ten percent, smaller than its original (`preview::worth_keeping`): an original already small and web-ready is shown as it is, and its storage is not spent on a copy no smaller. A poster is always far smaller than its video.

### Pictures

Pictures are made in the server's own process (`aspen_previews::picture::preview`, on a blocking thread), since the bytes are anyone's: decoded by the `image` crate's decoders, written in Rust (JPEG, PNG, WebP, GIF, TIFF, BMP), with an allocation limit, and refused before any of them is decoded when they hold more than `[media.previews] max_picture_pixels` pixels, as their header says, or their original more than `max_picture_bytes`. The pixel check comes before even the check for movement, since telling whether a GIF moves decodes its first two frames, each the size of its whole logical screen, which a GIF of a few bytes may declare as 65535 by 65535. Each is turned upright by its EXIF orientation, and brought into sRGB from the colour profile it carries (`moxcms`), so a phone's Display P3 photo keeps its colours; a picture without a profile is taken as sRGB. Resizing is Lanczos (`fast_image_resize`), and encoding is libwebp's (`webp`).

Some pictures keep their original: one that moves (an animated GIF, APNG, or WebP), since a preview would hold its first frame only; one whose colours cannot be brought into sRGB faithfully (a CMYK print file, a profile that cannot be read); and anything the decoders do not read, such as SVG or HEIC.

### Videos

A video's preview is its poster (`video::make`): the frame a second in, or the middle of a shorter video, taken by the operator's `ffmpeg` (`[media.previews] ffmpeg`, `ffprobe`), turned upright as it is decoded, and made into a picture's preview. Apps show it with a play control and play the original only when asked, so nothing of a video is fetched until the reader wants it.

Video is not transcoded. A minute of video costs minutes of every core a server has to encode, competing with everything else it serves, where a frame takes about a second however long the video. A video's poster is taken from a copy of the original in a scratch directory (under `TMPDIR`), by `ffmpeg` and `ffprobe` running as processes of their own, so a file that crashes a decoder or takes more memory than it should takes nothing else with it. They may read only that file (`-protocol_whitelist file`), as one of the containers videos are sent in (`-format_whitelist`, `video::CONTAINERS`), so a file made to look like a playlist cannot have them fetch addresses or read other files. They decode only the codecs such files hold (`-codec_whitelist`, `video::DECODERS`, sound and subtitles included, since reading a file's streams opens their decoders too), frames of at most `max_picture_pixels` (`-max_pixels`), on one thread; they run with none of the server's environment but `PATH`, and under limits set as they start (`video::limit`, `setrlimit` between fork and exec): `ffmpeg_memory_mib` of address space, a minute of CPU time, no file written, no core dump, and 64 open files. What they write is read only up to `max_picture_bytes` for the frame and a megabyte for `ffprobe`'s answer, the process killed past it; each run is stopped after a minute. HDR video has no poster, since its frame flattened to SDR without tone mapping would look washed out, and neither does a video larger than `max_video_bytes`.

A server on which `ffmpeg` or `ffprobe` will not run makes previews of pictures only, says so in its log when it starts, and leaves videos to the servers that can.

## Making them

Confirming an upload whose MIME type is a picture's or a video's queues its preview: a job (see Jobs), `makePicturePreview` or `makeVideoPoster`, keyed by the attachment, written in the transaction that confirms it (`preview::queue`), whose payload holds until when messages holding the attachment wait for it (`holdUntil`). Only servers whose `[media.previews]` has `make` on, and whose `[jobs]` has `run` on, claim them, and only those that can run `ffmpeg` claim `makeVideoPoster`; each makes at most `concurrency` previews at once, a bound the runner keeps for the two kinds together beside its own places (`jobs::bounded`). What was just uploaded is interactive; what the migration queued for pictures and videos uploaded before previews were made is bulk.

A preview that cannot be made now (storage did not answer) is tried again, waiting a minute and twice as long each time, six times (`preview::MAX_ATTEMPTS`), the messages it holds let go meanwhile; one that cannot be made at all, or is not worth keeping, is done with. A preview job no server has made within seven days (`preview::GIVEN_UP_AFTER`), as one may wait when no server can run `ffmpeg`, is deleted by `pruneFailedJobs`. A kept preview is stored at `attachment-previews/{id}` (beside the originals rather than under the original's own key, which some object stores cannot hold as both an object and a prefix), recorded on the attachment row (`preview_storage_key`, `preview_mime_type`, `preview_width`, `preview_height`, all or none), and announced, in one transaction that first locks the attachment row: `attachmentPreviewed`, naming the attachment, its message, and the preview, to the channel of each message holding the attachment, or to its uploader alone while it is in none. Locking first means a message taking up the attachment at the same moment is either seen by the announcement or written after it commits, its readers finding the preview on the attachment.

The attachment's record (`GET /attachments/{attachment}`, and every read that sideloads it) carries the preview as `preview`: its URL, MIME type, and size in pixels as it is shown upright. A preview is never taken away once made, so a client keeps one it heard of over a record read before it was made.

Deleting an attachment deletes its preview, and one being made as its attachment is deleted is deleted by its job, which finds the row gone; one whose attachment became evidence for reviewing reports meanwhile (its message deleted, or it taken off its message: `app::attachment::evidence`) is deleted the same way, its job deleted as it became evidence and the messages it held let go, and a preview already made moves to `evidence/` with its original. Purging a benchmark run purges its previews.

The metrics `aspen_attachment_previews_made_total`, `aspen_attachment_previews_failed_total`, and `aspen_attachment_preview_duration_seconds` count them, by `kind` (`picture` or `video`).

## Held messages

A message sent with an attachment whose preview is still being made would reach its readers showing the original inline, then switch to the preview as it landed. A client that shows its sender a message waiting says so with `mayHold: true` on `POST /channels/{channel}/messages`, and then such a message is held (`app::message::held`): checked as any message is (`app::message::check_posting`), kept in `held_message` with the language it was sent in, and answered `202 Accepted` with the held message, its id, channel, content, attachments, and when it was held. Without `mayHold`, as older clients and other deployments' send, a message is posted at once.

Each held message is posted by a job of its own (`releaseHeldMessage`, interactive, keyed by it; `held::release_step`), saved with it. Each preview job holds the messages its attachment is in until its `holdUntil`, twenty seconds after the upload (`preview::HOLD`), or until the preview is made, found not worth keeping, or fails, which sets `holdUntil` to now. A preview job that settles, in the transaction that records it, makes the jobs of the messages it held due at once (`held::wake_holding`, through `held_message_attachments_idx`); a job that finds its message still held looks again when the hold runs out, and at least every five seconds (`held::LOOK_EVERY`), and one whose author sent a message before it that is still held waits a second, so an author's held messages are posted in the order they were sent (through `held_message_by_author`). Once nothing holds it, it posts the message as it was sent (`app::message::post`), in its language, with the author's permissions as they are then and the channel's plugins deciding it then; the held row is deleted in the same transaction, so it is posted once however its job is run, and `heldMessagePosted` tells the author's apps which message it became. A preview finished after the hold ran out reaches the message's readers by `attachmentPreviewed`.

A held message that can no longer be posted (its author lost the right to post there, or was removed, or the channel went) is dropped, and `heldMessageFailed` tells the author's apps why, in the language it was sent in; one that fails for a reason that may pass is tried again every thirty seconds, ten times (`held::MAX_ATTEMPTS`), and dropped the same way on the last. `GET /users/@me/held-messages` lists the caller's held messages, oldest first, which their apps read as they start. A held message whose author's account or channel is deleted is dropped when it would be posted, since posting is checked then; the row goes with its user or channel only when those rows themselves are removed. A held first reply makes its thread as it is held, and dropping it removes the thread when nothing else was ever posted or waits there (see Threads and DMs).

A message held is posted after messages sent after it without a hold; held messages of one author go in the order they were sent.

## Unsent attachments

An attachment is meant to be sent: one confirmed a day ago (`app::attachment::UNSENT_LIFETIME`) that has never been in a message and is not held in one is deleted, row, object, and preview, by `attachment::sweep_unsent`, which every API server runs with the staging sweep (`media_store::spawn_upload_sweeper`, hourly), 500 at a time under `FOR UPDATE SKIP LOCKED`, so the store is not a file host for uploads nobody sends. `attachment.sent` records that an attachment has been in a message, set as it is put in one; one an edit has since taken out of its message is not swept. A message being saved locks its attachments (`FOR KEY SHARE`, in `message::ensure_attachments_ready`) so the sweep passes them by; an app that kept an upload in its composer for longer than a day finds it gone when it sends (`attachmentNotReady`) and uploads it again.

## When access is given or taken away

1. **Who can observe it, and by which routes?** A preview: whoever may read its attachment (`app::attachment::read_attachment`): its uploader, or whoever may view a channel holding it in a message not deleted, by REST reads of the attachment and its sideloads, and `attachmentPreviewed` on the subject of each message's channel (with `Aspen-Channel`) or the uploader's own. It is served on the same anonymous-read path as its original, under a key naming the same unguessable id. A held message: its author alone, by the `202`, `GET /users/@me/held-messages`, and `heldMessagePosted` and `heldMessageFailed` on their own subject.
2. **What decides it, and where is that checked?** A preview follows its attachment: the event's scope is `Message` for each message (`EventScope::Message`, routed by the channel's view permission), or `User` for the uploader, chosen in `preview::announce`; `expected_kind` pairs `attachmentPreviewed` with a channel scope when it names a message and the user's otherwise. A held message is checked as a message is when it is held and again when it is posted, by `check_posting` in the posting's transaction.
3. **When the deciding permission is lost, what happens to what is already open?** A reader who loses view of the channel loses the attachment as they lose the message, and receives no `attachmentPreviewed` after; a preview URL already read stays fetchable, as the original's does, since both are on the anonymous-read path, until the message is deleted or the attachment taken off it, when both move off that path within about five seconds (`app::attachment::evidence`). A held message whose author loses the right to post, or is removed, banned, or blocked by the DM's other person, is dropped when it would be posted, and only its author hears of it. A sign-out or a password change does not drop it: it was sent while signed in, as a message posted at once would have been. A deleted account's held messages are dropped when they would be posted.
4. **When it is gained, how does a client already open find out?** A preview is gained only by being made, which `attachmentPreviewed` announces; a reader newly let into a channel reads the attachments they lack, previews included.
5. **Does every path that changes it announce it?** Making a preview announces it in the transaction that records it. Deleting an attachment, by its uploader or by the sweep of unsent ones, is possible only while it is in no message, so nobody but its uploader held it, and nothing is announced. Posting and dropping a held message announce it in their transactions; a held message of a deleted account or channel is dropped, and announced, when it would be posted.
6. **Is it published inside the transaction that makes the change?** Yes. A posting that rolls back publishes `communityResync` for its community as any request's does (`app::events::settle` runs after each release).

`scripts/check_permissions.py` checks that a preview made in a channel a member may not view reaches them neither by event nor by REST, that a held message reaches nobody but its author until it is posted, that a held message whose author loses the right to post is dropped with its reason and is never seen, and that a dropped first reply takes the thread it made with it, while a dropped reply leaves a thread that has another.
